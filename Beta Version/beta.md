Milestone status: What did you complete for the beta from your original plan? What did you not complete? What did you do that was not part of your original plan?
- With the beta, we have a functioning .pd patch that combines all of our efforts. The depth parameter is present, allowing for the blend of the two resonator signals. Not present are full parameter controls for both resonators and rock type selection.

Surprises and challenges: What challenges and surprises did you encounter that affected your ability to progress according to plan?
- One challenge we encountered was that the resonators were initially sounding too pitched like tones instead of rock resonances. We found that adding more modes closer together in frequency counteracted this and produced a more natural sound, because the partials blurred together rather than standing out individually. This was also counteracted in the ductile resonator with a delay network and by introducing noise into the system.

Design and concept changes: Has your work thus far caused you to reassess any of your original concept, goals, deliverables, etc.? If so, explain what has changed and why.
- While adjusting parameters of the brittle resonator to get closer to the sound we wanted, we added some sliders for attack, decay, and cutoff frequency. We're thinking about expanding this approach so that users could control the entire patch through UI elements, with more options for parameters in addition to depth. A key part of the ductile resonator's design has been to add more triggers to the system. This was done by scanning noise for peaks after the exciter triggers the system and resonating those peaks rather than the exciter's peaks.

Technical changes: Has your work thus far caused you to reassess any elements of your technical toolset? If so, explain what has changed and why.
- For the brittle resonator, we switched from Pd’s bandpass filter to a biquad filter which let us set each mode's decay directly through its pole radius, independent of frequency. This required calculating the filter coefficients, for which we used an abstraction. We also normalize these coefficients to keep mode levels consistent. For the ductile resonator, we needed to add triggers to react more appropriately to the exciter. We also needed to add different methods of detuning the effects to get noisier outputs.

Remaining work: What are your "must-complete" tasks between now and the final version? What are your "stretch" goals that you will complete if time allows? Which group member is taking the lead on each item?
- The final version will include parameter controls for both resonators, alongside controls to futher manipulate the trigger model. A stretch goal could be incorporating resonator rock type "presets" and expanding sound design capabilities with more parameters and a custom UI.

- Our collaborative process has simplified the division of deliverables among our group. Because we have been working individually on Faultline's three components, we are each able to effectively devote our time to specific, agreed-upon tasks.
